Get Cited: 7 Claude Prompts to Put Your Product in AI Search

Your buyers ask AI what to buy, and AI reads Reddit. These are the 7 prompts I use to find the threads AI cites in my niche and show up in them. One tool, Claude, no copying threads by hand.

PROMPTS 7
TOOL Claude
TIME ~15 min
BY Carl — @StackLevels
How to use
  1. Open Claude (claude.ai) and turn on web search. Claude finds and reads the threads for you.
  2. Copy a prompt and replace the [brackets] with your product, buyer and subreddit.
  3. Run them in order, in the same chat. Each one builds on the last.
01Map

Find the subreddits AI cites

Start with where the answers already live. This becomes your map for everything else.

I sell [product], a [category] for [buyer]. Search the web and list the 10 questions my buyers ask before they buy. For each question, name the subreddits and Reddit threads where people get it answered, with links.
02Check

Check if you show up

See what a buyer sees today. Are you named? Who is, and where does that come from?

What are the best tools for [problem] for [buyer]? Compare the top options, say why each one made your list, and cite your sources.

Save this answer. You'll compare against it in prompt 07.

03Listen

Pull your buyers' exact words

The phrases people use on Reddit are the phrases AI repeats. Use them in everything you write.

Search r/[subreddit] for posts about [problem]. Quote the exact phrases people use to describe it, word for word, grouped by pain point.
04Rules

Know the rules before you post

Most active subreddits gate new accounts. Getting banned on day one costs you the whole channel.

Read the rules of r/[subreddit]. Tell me the account age and karma requirements, what counts as self-promotion there, and which kinds of posts get removed.
05Engage

Write a zero-link comment

Comments build the karma and trust you need before you post. No links, no brand drops.

Find a recent thread in r/[subreddit] about [problem]. Draft a reply as a [your role] who has dealt with this: one real experience, one practical step. No links, no product names, under 120 words, plain tone.

Edit it into your own words before you post. Reddit can tell.

06Publish

Write a post AI will quote

A real first-hand story beats a pitch. Lead with the answer.

Write a Reddit post for r/[subreddit] about my first-hand experience solving [problem]: what I tried, what worked and what didn't. Open with a 2-sentence direct answer, then the story, then one practical takeaway. Plain tone, no links. Disclose that I build [product].
07Measure

Monthly scorecard

Once a month, check whether it's working and what to post next.

Run prompts 1 and 2 again. Then compare with last month's answers: [paste last month's answers]. Was [product] or any of my posts cited? What changed, and what should I post next?

Doing this every day by hand gets old.
