One Question, Five Posts

Your buyers already told you what to post. Paste one real question into these 5 Claude prompts and get a week of posts, Monday to Friday, in your buyer's own words.

PROMPTS 5
USE Claude
BY Carl · @StackLevels
How to use
  1. Find one real question. Check your support inbox or read a competitor's reviews. Copy the buyer's exact words.
  2. Fill the [brackets] once: your product, your buyer, the problem, the question.
  3. Run one prompt per day, Monday to Friday, in the same Claude chat.
  4. Add what only you know at each [MY DETAIL]: a number from your product, a moment you watched happen, a mistake you made first.

5 Claude prompts, one per day

01Mon · Answer

The straight answer

Answer the question fully, as if they never click anything else.

I sell [product], which helps [buyer] with [problem]. A buyer asked this, in their own words:
"[paste the exact question]"

Write a post that answers it fully and directly. Start with the question as the first line, then the answer. Do not mention my product.

Keep their wording where you can. Plain words, short lines, one idea per line. No hashtags, no hype. Leave a gap marked [MY DETAIL] where I add a number, a moment or a mistake of my own.
02Tue · Mistake

The mistake behind it

Name the wrong move behind the question, kindly.

I sell [product], which helps [buyer] with [problem]. A buyer asked this, in their own words:
"[paste the exact question]"

Write a post about the mistake that usually leads someone to ask this. Name the mistake, show what it costs, then show the better move. No blame.

Keep their wording where you can. Plain words, short lines, one idea per line. No hashtags, no hype. Leave a gap marked [MY DETAIL] where I add a number, a moment or a mistake of my own.
03Wed · Story

Your story with it

Your own run-in with the same problem. Claude leaves the gap; you fill it.

I sell [product], which helps [buyer] with [problem]. A buyer asked this, in their own words:
"[paste the exact question]"

Write a short story post about the moment I hit this same problem and what I changed. Use [MY DETAIL] for every fact about me, so I fill in only what is true. End with the lesson in one line.

Keep their wording where you can. Plain words, short lines, one idea per line. No hashtags, no hype. Leave a gap marked [MY DETAIL] where I add a number, a moment or a mistake of my own.

Fill every [MY DETAIL] with something that really happened. Delete any line you can't fill.

04Thu · Myth

The myth around it

The belief that keeps people stuck, and what's true instead.

I sell [product], which helps [buyer] with [problem]. A buyer asked this, in their own words:
"[paste the exact question]"

Write a post that names the common belief behind this question, says plainly why it doesn't hold, and gives the reader what to do instead.

Keep their wording where you can. Plain words, short lines, one idea per line. No hashtags, no hype. Leave a gap marked [MY DETAIL] where I add a number, a moment or a mistake of my own.
05Fri · Steps

The steps

Close the week with something they can do today.

I sell [product], which helps [buyer] with [problem]. A buyer asked this, in their own words:
"[paste the exact question]"

Write a how-to post: 3 to 5 numbered steps that solve the problem in the question. Each step is one line the reader can act on. Mention my product only if one step truly needs a tool, and say I built it.

Keep their wording where you can. Plain words, short lines, one idea per line. No hashtags, no hype. Leave a gap marked [MY DETAIL] where I add a number, a moment or a mistake of my own.
What I learned

My most expensive mistake with my first app was not posting about it earlier. Don't wait for the perfect idea. Start with one buyer question this week.

Finding a fresh question every week takes reading.

That's why I'm building Nuureva: it watches where your buyers talk, surfaces the questions they ask and turns them into post ideas. You still write the posts.
